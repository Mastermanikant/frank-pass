While a random password generator is great for temporary accounts, Wi-Fi keys, or throwaway logins, relying on random strings for all your primary digital identities is fundamentally broken.

1. The Cognitive Impossibility of Random Gibberish

The human brain evolved to remember narratives, patterns, visual connections, and semantic meanings. It did not evolve to store fifty distinct 20-character strings like 7k#Q9!mP$2vL@zX. When humans are forced to use random strings, one of two things happens: they write them down in unencrypted notes, or they surrender their entire digital life to a cloud password manager.

If an adversary or state actor breaches that centralized cloud database, or compromises your master master-password, all your accounts across banking, email, cloud infrastructure, and social networks are exposed simultaneously.
