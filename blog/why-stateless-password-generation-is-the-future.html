What is a Stateless Password Generator, and why is it superior to cloud vaults?
A stateless password generator mathematically computes passwords on-demand using cryptographic key derivation functions (PBKDF2/HMAC-SHA512) seeded by your private master secret and the website domain. Unlike traditional cloud password managers (1Password, Bitwarden, LastPass) that store encrypted vaults on central servers, a stateless generator stores zero bytes of data anywhere in the universe. With no server database to breach, it eliminates 100% of centralized vault leaks, works 100% offline, and achieves mathematical unhackability.

2. The Paradigm Shift: What is Stateless Deterministic Cryptography?

Stateless cryptography eliminates the database entirely.

Instead of acting like a digital locker that stores passwords on a disk, a stateless generator operates like a pure mathematical function.

$$\text{Password} = f(\text{Master Secret Passphrase}, \text{Domain Name}, \text{Context Salt})$$

The Law of Determinism

In mathematics, a deterministic function has a fundamental property: Given the exact same input, it will ALWAYS produce the exact same output, with zero variance, across any computer in the universe.

$$2 + 2 = 4 \quad \text{(Always, everywhere, for all eternity)}$$

Similarly, in FrankPass, when you provide your Master Secret Passphrase (e.g. "Newton's country is the best country") and a target domain (e.g. google.com), FrankPass executes a multi-stage cryptographic pipeline:

  1. Your Secret Key is normalized (lowercase + strip all whitespace).
  2. An intermediate cryptographic pepper is derived via HMAC-SHA512 and 1,000 rounds of SHA-256 stretching.
  3. This pepper enters PBKDF2 with 1,000,000 rounds of HMAC-SHA512, producing a 512-bit key.
  4. A final HMAC expansion generates the deterministic byte stream that maps to your password.

The resulting 20-character password is calculated entirely inside your device's RAM in under 2–3 seconds (the deliberate computational delay of 1,000,000 PBKDF2 rounds is by design-it makes brute-forcing astronomically expensive for attackers). As soon as you copy it or close the tab, the RAM is instantly purged.

Zero bytes are written to a hard drive. Zero bytes are transmitted across the internet.

The Entropy Equation

The strength of a cryptographic hash is measured in bits of search entropy ($H$):

$$H = L \cdot \log_2(N)$$

Where $L$ is the length of the generated password (e.g., 20 characters) and $N$ is the character set size (e.g., 55 unambiguous characters in FrankPass):

$$H = 20 \cdot \log_2(55) \approx 20 \cdot 5.78 \approx 115.6 \text{ bits of pure cryptographic entropy}$$

To crack a 115-bit PBKDF2 hash with 1,000,000 rounds of HMAC-SHA512 would require $2^{115}$ computational attempts. Even if an attacker harnessed all the supercomputers on planet Earth consuming the entire electrical output of the sun, brute-forcing it would take trillions of years.
